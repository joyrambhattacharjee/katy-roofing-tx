Where We Work

Katy sits where Fort Bend, Harris, and Waller counties meet — which is why a hailstorm on one side of town can leave the other side untouched. Every community we serve gets its own read: age of housing stock, tree cover, storm history, and what that means for the roof decisions people there are actually facing.

What Happens After You Reach Out

You'll hear back the same business day in most cases. We'll ask a few questions — roof age, what you've noticed, whether there's an active leak — and either schedule an inspection or answer the question outright. Inspections are documented: photos of the roof, a written summary of what we found, and a plain explanation of your options. If a repair is the right call, you'll get a repair scope. If it isn't, you'll get the photos that show why.

If your request is storm-related — hail within the last few days, a limb through the roof, active water coming in — say so in the form or on the phone. Those get moved to the front of the schedule, and we'll walk you through the temporary measures that limit damage until a crew can get there.

Active Leak? Call, Don't Form.

Water in the house moves faster than email. Phone gets a human, and storm calls are answered after hours.

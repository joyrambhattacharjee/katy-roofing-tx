Commercial Roofing Systems We Install and Service

TPO — the default single-ply for new Katy commercial work: heat-welded seams, reflective white surface that fights our heat, good economics on large square footage. PVC — similar welding and reflectivity with stronger chemical resistance, the right call where exhaust stacks or kitchen vents dump onto the roof. EPDM — black rubber membranes with decades of track record on older buildings; often the sensible repair-and-restore choice rather than a tear-off. Modified bitumen — built-up asphalt systems for roofs that see heavy traffic, equipment loads, or extra abuse. Coatings and restoration — silicone and acrylic systems that renew an aging membrane, add reflectivity, and defer replacement capital by years when the substrate is still sound.

We match the system to the building, not the other way around — roof slope, deck type, foot traffic, equipment weight, and what's realistic for your budget cycle.

Where Commercial Roofs Actually Fail

Commercial roofing lives and dies at the details, and Katy's inventory of distribution centers, retail boxes, and office parks shows every one of them. Seams and terminations — single-ply seams that were never properly welded pull apart under thermal cycling. Penetrations — every pipe, conduit, and vent stack through the membrane is a leak waiting for aging sealant. Drains and scuppers — a Katy downpour dumps inches in an afternoon; one clogged interior drain ponds water until it finds a seam. Curbs and rooftop equipment — HVAC units, satellite mounts, and service walkways damage membranes at their flashing and vibration points. ponding itself, which turns any small defect into a big one by holding water against it for days.

Our inspections evaluate all of it — membrane condition, seam integrity, drainage performance, flashing and pitch pockets — with a written report you can hand to ownership or plan capital around.

Maintenance Programs for Property Owners

Commercial roofs don't fail suddenly; they fail on a schedule you can read in advance. A recurring maintenance program — scheduled inspections, drain clearing, seam and sealant touch-ups, minor repairs before they compound — stretches roof life and turns "surprise replacement" into "planned line item."

For multi-tenant retail and office property, that matters twice: tenants don't tolerate stained ceiling tiles, and deferred roof problems show up in lease renewals as often as in repair invoices. We document everything, so the file on your roof is always current and always yours.

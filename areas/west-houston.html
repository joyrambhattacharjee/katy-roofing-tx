Roofing in West Houston: What We See

Everything west of the Energy Corridor — the Memorial-adjacent office parks, the medical and professional buildings along I-10, the retail fronting the freeway — runs low-slope commercial roofing, and most of it is TPO or aging PVC and EPDM. What those roofs have in common is traffic: HVAC service visits, satellite installs, signage contractors, all walking the membrane in work boots. The damage we get called for is almost never the field of the roof; it's the seams opened by thermal cycling, the flashing around the latest unit swap, the pitch pocket somebody topped off with the wrong sealant.

Residential West Houston is the quieter half: mature neighborhoods from the 1970s-2000s where the roofing stock spans every era of shingle, big tree canopies that mean shade, moss, and limb damage in the same storm, and drainage patterns that were designed before several decades of settlement shifted where water goes. Those roofs reward an inspection that looks at ventilation and drainage, not just shingles.
